The 10-Second Photon Rocket

Easy STEM Space Project

Imagine holding a rocket engine that does not use fire, smoke, or heavy fuel. Instead, it shoots trillions of tiny light particles—called photons—straight into space!

Real space scientists are studying this technology right now to fly spacecraft to distant stars at the speed of light. Today, you will build a miniature model. It takes exactly three items, zero wires, and 10 seconds to build.

How to Connect the Pieces

Long Leg (+) 👉 Smooth Flat Side (+) of Battery

Short Leg (-) 👉 Bumpy Textured Side of Battery

Step-by-Step Instructions

Step 1
Check the Legs

Hold your tiny LED bulb. Notice that one metal wire leg is slightly longer than the other.

Step 2
Place the Battery

Slide your coin battery right between the two metal wire legs.

Step 3
Pinch to Light Up

Squeeze the long wire leg against the flat, smooth side of the battery (the "+" side). Touch the short leg to the bumpy back side. The bulb will turn on instantly!

Step 4
Lock it Together

Wrap a piece of duct tape tightly around the whole thing. The tape keeps the wires pressed against the battery so the light stays on by itself.

Go into a dark room, point your rocket at the ceiling, and watch your light beam travel into the night!

Safety Rules for Parents

1. Battery Danger: Coin batteries are a severe hazard if swallowed. Keep loose pieces away from small children and pets.

2. Adult Supervision: An adult must help with this project. Once the light works, an adult should tape the parts securely so they cannot come loose.

3. Electrical Safety: The 3V battery is safe to touch and will not shock you. Never try to plug LED bulbs into wall outlets.

4. Disclaimer: This project is for educational use. By building it, you assume all responsibility for safety.
